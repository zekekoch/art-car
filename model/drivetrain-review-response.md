## What changed in the design

The review's findings pushed four real geometry changes, not just wording:

1. **Steering layout.** `frame-3d` now solves each wheel from a fixed-length tie rod (nearest root; "no solution" when a rod cannot close) instead of assigning both knuckles one angle, and it sweeps the rack to find lock against the arm legs, spring, side rail and receiver sleeves using the tire's chord at each obstacle's height. Doing that exposed two things the old animation hid: with the rails 12" in from the edge the tire chord at rail-bottom height met the rail at ~10° of steer, and a rack behind the axle 26" from the arm pivot axis gave −11.6° of toe at 3" of travel. The layout is now: rails 14" in (24" apart), steering arms pointing forward and outward from the kingpins 3" above the hub centre, and the rack on the frame at the ball joints' own station and height, so the transverse rods are square to the ball joints' arc. Result: 22° inner / 21° outer at ±2.5" of rack travel (ideal Ackermann outer for that inner is 19.4°, so 1.5° under), ~15.5 ft to the outside tire, toe change 1.4° over ±3" (second order; a centre-take-off rack with pivots near the centreline brings it to ~1°). `drivetrain-3d` carries the same layout and its verification checks it.
2. **Kingpin.** 8" inboard of the hub centre plane (from 10"), which is about the least this single-sided package allows (knuckle body, torque clamp, shaft and thread, motor face), inclined 12° so the axis meets the ground ~5.6" inboard of the contact centre. Kingpin 1¾"; bending at a 1,350 lb corner with 600 lb of side load is ~14,300 in·lb → 27 ksi. The front arm's two legs now both sit inboard of the kingpin plane (4.5" and 10"), because a leg beside the tire limits lock to ~14°.
3. **Kneel.** Arms articulate about their pivots in `frame-3d`; the bag height is measured from the geometry (6.25" installed, D2500 at 7" diameter). Kneel available is **5.6"**, not 8: the bag bottoms at 6.0" through the 0.625 motion ratio, the tire chord meets the receiver sleeves at 5.6" as the axle walks ~1" toward the pivot, the sled is 3" off the ground at 6.5", a 6" fender box stops the tire at 7". Receivers went from 26" to 28" apart for this. Dampers, bump stops and droop straps are drawn as reserved envelopes.
4. **Pod receivers.** Moments are now taken about the rail face (12" inboard of the pod centre line): 12,500 in·lb static per passenger pod, 37,500 design (×3), 14,700 static for the driver pod about the end rail. The 2×2×0.188" arms run 25 ksi (53% of yield) at that; the default is now **2×3×0.188" on edge** (14 ksi, 30%) in 2½ × 3½ receivers, +5 lb per pod. The models keep 2×2×0.188 and 2×2×0.250 selectable.

## Still open, in the order they block the build

1. QS205 car-motor drawing and wheel-end load rating for the exact variant; continuous torque and temperature at 75–85 rpm; a rim that matches CB60 / 4×100 without re-drilling.
2. Stair handrail on the stair module (the DMV requires a secure railing on any stairs) and a loading/unloading procedure for the inspection; the 26" rims and 12" gate are not licensing items at a 24" floor.
3. Trailer loading plan against the 5,000 lb tow rating: what rides in the RV (sled, ottomans, stairs, panels) and the hitch's tongue rating.
4. Spring choice for the crowded-rear corner load (D2600 or seat nearer the axle) and the resulting kneel.
5. Brake sizing and a matched rotor/caliper set for the hub; rack selection with ~5" of travel.
6. Receiver joint free-body check and a jig load test of the first pod.
7. First physical prototype: one front corner plus a rack fixture, loaded to the corner reactions and cycled through travel and lock, before the chassis is frozen. Then the low-speed motor test.
