Plan of record · electrical · rev 13 September 2026

Electrical

One 48 V bus for everything on the drive side, fed by a LiFePO4 battery sled that rolls under the car; 12 V rails per lighting zone through DC-DC converters. Four motor controllers, one per corner; roughly 5,000 addressable LEDs across the pods and the roof rim; 1.2 kW of solar on the roof; a generator at camp for the nightly recharge.

What has been decided

  • Battery: new LiFePO4 prismatic cells. EVE LF280K (3.2 V / 280 Ah, ~0.9 kWh, 5.4 kg each), 16S per string, two independent strings — 32 cells, 28.7 kWh nominal, 22.9–25.8 kWh usable in an 80–90 % window; 51.2 V nominal, 58.4 V at 3.65 V per cell. Used Nissan Leaf modules were considered and set aside (heat-sensitive pouch cells under a deck at 105 °F, worse on price per real kWh, weight and volume). 314 Ah cells now sell at the same price, so ~33 kWh for the same money. decided cell revision
  • The sled. Cells, BMS, contactors, fusing and DC-DC in one rolling unit reserved at 50 × 21 × 10.5″ (~475 lb) that positions under the car's centreline, 5″ rearward of centre, and bolts up with four bolts, two Anderson SB350 plugs and one comms plug; the car kneels onto it. It also charges at home off the car, and rides in the RV rather than on the trailer. A precharge circuit in the sled so a hot 48 V plug does not arc into the controller capacitors. decided
  • Controllers. Four 48 V sine-wave FOC controllers, one per motor, for torque split; the candidate is the Kelly KLS4830S (30–62 V, 120 A continuous, 300 A one-minute boost), with CAN or serial out so a small board can read speed and current per corner. Regen into a full pack needs a firmware ceiling because 58.4 V leaves little margin under 62 V. direction
  • Redundancy is a feature to build, not a free consequence of two strings. Each string's protection, contactor and current sharing must be designed for one string dropping out under load, and the controllers told about it. direction
  • Driver interface. F / N / R lever wired to the controllers' direction and enable lines; neutral disables the controllers so the motors freewheel. Lighting panel and pack readout on the driver's right console. decided
  • Lighting. Every module is its own zone with one connector: twelve light boxes per pod (4 LED rows each, ~530 WS2812 per pod, ~45 W typical, ~160 W at full white), a mirror-skirt strip under each pod floor, and two rows in the roof rim (~1,700 LEDs). Controller, power distribution and harness terminals live inside each pod's end consoles. The current car's driver is a custom Teensy 3.2 board running FastLED (QueenFX), 16 parallel outputs at ~273 LEDs each; that code base is the starting point. direction
  • Air management. 12 V compressor, 2 gal tank, four solenoid valves, four pressure and four height sensors. Pressure tracks corner load only after calibration at a known height — a tip warning needs that plus the CG estimate the chassis model makes; it is not a load cell. direction
  • Solar. Twelve 100 W flex panels on the roof leaves, ~1.2 kW nominal, worth 4–6 kWh on a good day. Treated as a bonus until measured. direction
  • Charging. Nightly at camp from a Honda EU3200i (2.6 kW rated) through a 48 V 40 A charger (~2.2 kW into the pack): 9–11 hours and 3–4 gallons to replace 19–25 kWh, so the generator runs essentially all day. Two in parallel, or a larger camp generator, halves it. direction

Net 4.4–16 kWh per day before conversion losses and reserve. With 23–26 kWh usable that is nightly charging at the top of the range and every other day at the bottom: plan the camp shore cord for nightly. Range per charge with 12 aboard and 6 kWh of lights: 40 / 21 / 12 miles at Crr 0.03 / 0.06 / 0.10 (0.46 / 0.90 / 1.49 kWh per mile). A night of cruising is 10–20 miles, so the middle case works with nightly charging and the soft-playa case does not. The two unmeasured numbers are rolling resistance on the playa and motor efficiency at 84 rpm; one night with a Victron SmartShunt on the current car's pack and a phone GPS track gives Wh per mile all-in.

Open items

  1. EVE has shipped several LF280K revisions with different current ratings; confirm the purchased revision before sizing fusing and contactors. The 50 × 21 × 10.5″ sled is a packaging allowance, not an enclosure design.
  2. Peak power: a single 16S string tops out around 7 kW continuous, tight for four motors in soft playa with ten aboard; the two-string design has to actually deliver that and the limp-home behaviour.
  3. Controller tuning, BMS/regen coordination (the 62 V ceiling), and the per-corner torque split logic.
  4. Lighting controller for the new car: how many outputs, where it lives, and the connector standard for pods and modules.
  5. Measure Crr and Wh per mile on the current car this year (SmartShunt + GPS; crane scale on a tow rope).

How we got here

Section 4 of the design notebook has the cell comparison (15 / 30 / 45 kWh), the used-Leaf-module analysis, and the energy budget and range arithmetic as corrected after the drivetrain review (the first version had the lighting line wrong by a factor of ~2.5, a single string, and a battery box with no room for walls). The current car's lighting — ~3,300 WS2815 on a custom Teensy board running FastLED — is the baseline the notebook's energy table uses, and the code base the new car starts from.
